Backdrop

No camera to hand? Trace on a photograph instead. Take it from where the camera will stand on the night and the shapes will land close enough to nudge into place.

Square up the wall

The camera sees the wall at an angle, and anything the app generates — brickwork, grids, anything that tiles — inherits that angle. Mark something you know is rectangular and say what shape it really is, and generated texture becomes uniform on the building instead of on the picture.

Any units you like, as long as both are the same — metres, bricks, courses, panes. Only the ratio matters.

Depth scan

Walk round the front of the house with a scanning app — Polycam, Scaniverse, anything that exports a .glb — and the app can find the windows itself and light the building’s real surface instead of painting on it. Export with compression turned off.

Point at the same thing in both pictures. The scan’s own corners are blank render and sky with nothing to line them up against, and a scan that stops short of the roof has its top edge across the middle of the house — so features are the only reliable thing to aim at.

How far out of the wall something has to stand before it counts as a feature, in millimetres. Twenty clears brickwork and render; a window reveal gives you fifty to a hundred.

The sound of the show

Water lapping, legs skittering, thunder on the flash. Synthesised from whatever layers are on, and played here — this is the machine with the speakers. Each layer's own level is under it in the inspector and on the remote; where several layers make the same noise, one of them carries it and holds the fader.
